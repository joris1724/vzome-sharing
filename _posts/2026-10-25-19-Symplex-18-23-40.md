<a href="https://www3.mpifr-bonn.mpg.de/staff/pfreire/polyhedra/index.html">Paulo's Freire's site on polytope models</a> is for me the primary source on polytopes of various dimensions and their relations. In <a href="https://www3.mpifr-bonn.mpg.de/staff/pfreire/polyhedra/beyond_a.htm">section 10</a> Paulo demonstrates on <a href="https://www.nan.ma/zomeable-4polytopes/output/gosset_projections/7_11_page2.html">Nan Ma's vZome model</a> that the icosahedral projection of the 10-Orthoplex (Gosset's 7_11) can be <i>simply</i> extended to the dodecahedral projection of the 19-Simplex by adding <b>radial struts</b>.

The building instructions show that these models are easy to build, in six steps starting from an Icosahedron. The 19-Simplex only has 20 vertices. In the Zometool model, these will be represented by <b>white</b> connectors. The icosahedral projection of the 10-Orthoplex is composed by blue- and green-orbit struts. The addition of the yellow-orbit struts completes the model of the 10-Simplex. Due to the projection to 3D, a lot of edges either everlap or intersect. These intersections have been replaced in the vZome model by <b>false vertices</b>, represented by <b>yellow</b>, <b>green</b> or <b>blue</b> connectors:

However, the crosses of g1's introduced in Step&nbsp;5 with <b>green</b> connectors are not Zometool-able. Firstly, the ratio of long and short branches is non-standard (2+&phi;:3-&phi;). More importantly, the tips of the long branches of two different crosses would be put simultaneously in the same pentagonal hole of a Zometool ball. 3D printing is used to handle these problems. A <a href="https://joris1724.github.io/vzome-sharing/2026/10/25/18-23-40-19-Symplex/19-Symplex.scad">monolithical cross</a> has been designed, eliminating the <b>green</b> false connectors and redirecting the tips of the long branches to neigbouring triangular holes.

The feet of the long branches are excavated drastically to allow the neighbouring standard Zometool blue struts to connect to their rectangular holes. By this the crosses have been printed in PETG, rather than PLA. The crosses of four hg0's introduced in Step&nbsp;2 of the building instructions are definitely Zometool-able. For the physical model however, I also replaced these by monolithically 3D printed objects, in order to avoid the <b>yellow</b> false connectors:

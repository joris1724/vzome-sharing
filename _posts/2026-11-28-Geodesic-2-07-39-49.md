There are two Icosahedron Frequency-2 Spheres: the <i>Class I</i> {3,5+}<sub>2,0</sub> (Conway notation <b>dcdI</b>, <b>d</b>ualed<b> c</b>hamfered <b>d</b>ualed<b> <b>I</b>cosahedron) and the <i>Class II</i> {3,5+}<sub>1,1</sub> (Conway notation <b>kD</b>, <b>k</b>issed <b>D</b>odecahedron). Both have two types of edges. The first scene in the following vZome model shows that the <i>Class I</i> looks like a Rhombic Triacontahedron whose faces are replaced by triangle pairs, and that the <i>Class II</i> looks like a Icosadodecahedron whose pentagonal faces are replaced by pentagonal pyramids. The seconds scene displays the effect of rotation (here by 36°) of each of the edges, obtaining model coloured by piece type. These do not look so nice as in the last scene, using a 5-coloured scheme in which no piece touches another piece of the same colour.

Building the physical model of the <i>Class I</i> Frequency-2 (having 120 sticks and only 2 piece types) is less challenging than of its <a href="https://joris1724.github.io/vzome-sharing/2026/11/22/Geodesic-3-0-23-21-00.html">Frequency-3 counterpart</a> (having 270 sticks and 5 piece types). Because of the 5-colour scheme, the construction is certainly not obvious. Moreover one has to cope with the ever increasing tensions wanting to rip apart the model until it is finally closed. The <i>Class II</i> Frequency-2 is even a bit easier to build, having only 90 sticks. Also its second piece type is rotationally invariant.

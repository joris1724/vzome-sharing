<a href="">Derek Bosch</a> made a model of the <i>Class I</i> Icosahedron Frequency-3 Sphere (geodesic notation {3,5+}<sub>3,0</sub>) without using connectors (such as Zometool balls). The 270 edges are supposed to held themselves together by tension alone. The scenes in the following vZome model try to demonstrate how is constructed. The first scene show the vertices and edges of the {3,5+}<sub>3,0</sub>. There are five different types of edges. To obtain the coordinates of the vertices, I feeded the Conway notation of the {3,5+}<sub>3,0</sub>, <b>ktI</b> (<b>k</b>issed <b>t</b>runcated <b>I</b>cosahedron), into Anselm Levskaya's <a>polyHédronisme generator</a>, and exported as a VRML2 model. This allows to parse the coordinates and to rotate the model alligned with the Zome axes. To force the edges to support each other, they have to be rotated <i>a bit</b>, so that their endpoints can be bended under neighbouring edges, and that they can pass over two neighbouring edges themselves. In the vZome model, I used a rotation of 30° for all edges. The subsequent scenes in the vZome model illustrate the rotation applied on the edges of a specific type. The penultimate scene gathers all these results into a model in which the edges are coloured by piece type. The last scene adopts a much nicer colouring scheme whereby no piece touches another piece of the same colour.

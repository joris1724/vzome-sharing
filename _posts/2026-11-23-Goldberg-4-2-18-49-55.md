As a tribute to Derek Bosch's <a href="https://joris1724.github.io/vzome-sharing/2026/11/22/Geodesic-3-0-23-21-00.html">Icosahedron Frequency-3 Sphere puzzle</a> designed the "<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2cpxqiWQZJA">Spheregrite 840</a>, a Frequency-6 sphere. He reverse engineered Derek's design and <i>somehow</i> arrived modelling the Icosahedral Goldberg Sphere GP<sub>5</sub>(4,2) (geodesic notation {5,3+}<sub>4,2</sub>). As indicated by the notation, the {5,3+}<sub>4,2</sub> is the dual of the Icosahedral Geodesic Sphere {3,5+}<sub>4,2</sub>. Both are <i>Class III</i> Frequency-6 objects, differing from the corresponding regular <i>Class I</i> Frequency-6 objects by the way one moves from one pentagon to a neigbouring one: the (6,0)-versions take six steps in the same direction while the (4,2)-versions take four steps in one direction, then turn 60° before taking another two steps. The 840 edges are supposed to held themselves together by tension alone. The scenes in the following vZome model try to demonstrate how the <i>Spheregrite 840</i> is constructed. The first scene show the vertices and edges of the GP<sub>5</sub>(4,2). There are 14 different types of edges. To obtain the coordinates of the vertices, I feeded the Conway notation of the GP<sub>5</sub>(4,2), <b>wcD</b> (<b>w</b>hirled <b>c</b>hamfered <b>D</b>odecahedron), into Anselm Levskaya's <a>polyHédronisme generator</a>, and exported as a VRML2 model. This allows to parse the coordinates and to rotate the model alligned with the Zome axes. To force the edges to support each other, they have to be rotated <i>a bit</b>, so that their endpoints can be bended under neighbouring edges, and that they can pass over two neighbouring edges themselves. In the vZome model, I used a rotation of 20° for all edges. The subsequent scenes in the vZome model illustrate the rotation applied on the edges of a specific type. The last scene gathers all these results into a model in which the edges are coloured by piece type.
